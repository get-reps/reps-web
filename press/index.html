About REPS

REPS in 50 and 150 words

Copy either version as it is.

Short (50 words)

REPS is an iOS app that helps people remember what they read, watch and hear. Save an article, a YouTube video, a podcast, a PDF or a page from a book, and REPS turns it into quick daily games built from your own material, so what you save stays with you.

Long (150 words)

REPS is an iOS app that helps people remember what they read, watch and hear, with no notes or folders to keep.

Saving takes seconds. Share an article, a YouTube video, a podcast episode or a screenshot from any app, or add a PDF or a photo of your bookshelf in REPS, and REPS does the rest. It picks out the ideas worth keeping and brings them back over the following days and weeks as short daily games made from your own material, timed using spaced repetition. Games include a crossword, a cryptic, Quotle, a daily quote game, and trivia against a friend. Everything you save also builds Your Second Brain, an interactive map of what you know.

REPS was founded by Mike McGraw, who spent twelve years as an investor, most recently in venture capital, before leaving to build it full time. It is free on the App Store.

The facts

What it is
An app that turns what you read, watch and hear into quick daily games, so you remember it.
What you can save
Web articles, YouTube videos, podcast episodes, PDFs and documents, screenshots and photos, a photo of a bookshelf or a book page, reading lists, and your own notes.
How it works
Share from any app, or save in REPS. Each day REPS brings back what you saved as a short game, spaced over days and weeks. Games include Ladder, Crossword, Cryptic, Quotle and Friends Trivia.
Platform
iOS, on the App Store. A Chrome extension saves from your desktop browser into the app. There is no Android or web version.

Short bio

Long bio

Mike McGraw is the founder of REPS. He grew up between two kinds of entrepreneur: his mother has run a magazine business in Quebec for 25 years, and his father's startup went bankrupt while Mike was a student at McGill University.

He spent the next twelve years in investing. He started in natural resources at PSP Investments, helped launch the technology investment team at CDPQ, and then joined Inovia, where he became a Principal and moved to help set up the firm's European office.

In 2025 he went deep on AI, spending his spare time building with language models and automation tools. At the same time he was watching his nephews and nieces grow up on endless short videos. REPS came out of both: the idea that the mechanics that keep people scrolling could instead help them keep what they learn. He built it on nights and early mornings alongside his job, and left Inovia on 30 June 2026, his 34th birthday, to work on it full time.
