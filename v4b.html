How it works

From saved to known.

1

Save it from anywhere.

Tap Share in Safari, YouTube, Spotify, Instagram or Apple Podcasts. Add a PDF, a screenshot, or a photo of your bookshelf, or search a book by its title. It takes seconds, and REPS does the rest.

Saved in this clip 1
  1. SafariThe Beatles, on Wikipedia
  2. YouTubea Short
  3. Spotifya podcast episode
  4. Instagraman economics Reel
  5. Apple Podcastsan episode
2

Keep the ideas you want.

REPS reads what you saved and pulls out the ideas. You choose the ones worth keeping. It’s your library, not someone else’s course.

3

Play a few minutes a day.

A short daily game built from your own saves: multiple choice, true or false, fill in the blank, or say it back in your own words. Each idea returns just before it would fade, over days and then weeks.

Five more games, same library

  • Ladder
  • Crossword
  • Cryptic
  • Quotle
  • Friends Trivia

Friends Trivia: play a friend on the same round and compare scores.

Also in the app Ask Echo about anything you’ve saved. Voice rounds in English, French, German, Spanish, Italian and Portuguese. A Lock Screen widget, reminders, streaks, friends and a leaderboard.

Somewhere along the way, you stop counting.

The book you finished in spring is still with you in autumn. You become the one who remembers it: what went wrong on Everest in 1996, what Orwell meant by doublethink, which idea from last month’s podcast actually holds up.

From the founder

Mike McGraw watched his nephews and nieces grow up on YouTube Shorts, read that the Flynn effect has reversed, and came to think Huxley, not Orwell, had called it: control through entertainment that asks nothing of you. In 2025 he deleted Instagram and went back to books, long reads and documentaries. On 30 June 2026, his 34th birthday, he left twelve years of technology investing, at CDPQ and then Inovia Capital, to build REPS full-time. He uses it every day.
