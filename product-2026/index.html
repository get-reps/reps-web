The Daily Habit

Five minutes. Real retention.

A daily session built on SM-2 spaced repetition. Engineered to feel like a game, not a flashcard app.
  • 01
    Adaptive queueThe algorithm picks what you're closest to forgetting. You never review what you already know.

    Multi-format repsMultiple choice, fill-in-blank, true/false today. Recall, reflection, and synthesis formats on the way. The variety keeps the brain engaged and the habit sticky.

The Quiet Revolution in How We Remember
A new generation of readers is choosing to save, not share. The implications for publishers are larger than they first appear.
By Anya Mehta · May 28, 2026
Readers increasingly distinguish between content worth broadcasting and content worth keeping. Illustration by Jules Pellegrini

For two decades, digital publishers have optimized for one signal above all others: the click. Every headline, every push notification, every paywall prompt has been engineered to win a momentary act of attention.

But a quieter pattern is beginning to emerge. A reader who saves a piece for themselves behaves nothing like a reader who shares it with their followers, and publishers are only just starting to notice.
