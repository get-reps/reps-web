Summaries versus being asked

A summary helps you find and review the main points, but reading it again is not the same as being asked. Being asked, then asked again a few days later, is the idea behind active recall and spaced repetition. This page is about video and audio; for books and articles, see our guide to remembering what you read.

The six apps

REPS: the ideas you pick come back as a game

Our pick. Save a YouTube video, an Instagram or TikTok link, or a podcast episode from Spotify or Apple Podcasts. REPS pulls out the ideas worth keeping, you tap the ones you want, now or later, and they come back a few minutes a day as a game, timed with spaced repetition. Once three saves are ready, Ladder, Crossword, Cryptic and Friends Trivia open up, and the first three are built from what you saved (REPS facts). A Discover feed lists new articles to explore, each showing how many concepts it holds. You can also read back a written summary of each save.

Recall: the most complete AI knowledge base

The all-rounder. Saves and summarizes YouTube videos, podcasts, articles and PDFs, on the web, iOS, Android and four browsers. The free plan covers saving, notes and 10 AI summaries a month; Plus adds unlimited summaries, the automatic graph, chat and quizzes in seven formats that you generate per card and choose to schedule; Max adds the choice of AI model (pricing, quiz docs). See REPS vs Recall.

Recl: video and reel summaries that come back

The video summarizer. Summarizes YouTube videos, reels, TikToks, X posts and articles, with chapter timestamps for YouTube, brings each save back after 1, 3, 7, 14, 30 and 60 days (one tap marks it reviewed) and writes a weekly quiz. iPhone, iPad, Android, web, Chrome and Edge, in 20 languages (Recl). See REPS vs Recl.

Snipd: the best way to capture podcasts

The podcast pick. A podcast player for learning: tap your headphones to save a snip with the audio, transcript and summary, and its Daily recap brings snips back with spaced repetition. The free plan has AI features on 2 episodes a week; chat and YouTube or audio imports are on Premium. Snipd does not describe quizzing you (Snipd, pricing).

Readwise Reader: highlight video transcripts

The reader. Saves articles, PDFs, EPUBs, newsletters and YouTube videos, and lets you highlight a transcript as you watch. Readwise brings highlights back in a Daily Review, with Mastery flashcards on the Highlights and Full plans; 30-day free trial. Only what you highlight comes back (Reader, pricing).

Gemini Notebook: ask questions about your sources

The research notebook. Google's tool, previously NotebookLM, takes public YouTube links, audio files, PDFs and more, answers questions, builds mind maps and Audio Overviews, and makes flashcards or quizzes when you ask. Each notebook stands alone, and Google's help does not describe a review schedule (sources, flashcards and quizzes).

Where REPS fits better

Where REPS is weaker

  • The app is for iPhone and iPad, plus a Chrome extension for saving: no Android app, no web app, and no offline use.
  • No library imports from other apps, only a reading list from a screenshot or a Goodreads export.
FAQ

Frequently asked

What is the best app to remember what you watch on YouTube?

It depends on what you want back. For a written summary of a video, Recall, Recl and REPS all offer one you can read back, and Recl adds chapter timestamps. REPS also turns the ideas you pick from a saved video into questions in a short daily game, so you are tested on them until you remember.

Is there an app that quizzes you on podcasts?

Yes. REPS takes podcast episode links from Spotify, Apple Podcasts or a feed and turns the ideas you pick into questions. Recall can generate quizzes from a saved episode on its paid Plus plan. Snipd's Daily recap brings your snips back with spaced repetition, though Snipd does not describe quizzes.

Can AI summaries help me remember a video?

A summary helps you find and review the main points, but reading it again is not the same as being asked. Apps that combine both, like Recall with scheduled quizzes or REPS, cover more of the job.

Is there a free app to remember what I watch?

Snipd has a free plan with AI features on 2 episodes a week. Recall has a free plan with 10 AI summaries a month; its quizzes are on the paid Plus plan. Gemini Notebook has a free tier with a Google account. Recl is free to try on mobile with 3 saves. REPS is currently free on the App Store.

Which of these work on Android?

Recall, Recl, Readwise Reader and Gemini Notebook have Android apps. REPS has no Android app: it is an iPhone and iPad app with a Chrome extension for saving. Check Snipd's site for its current platforms.
