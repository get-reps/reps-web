The best Recall alternatives, by what you want instead (2026)

By Mike McGraw, founder of REPS · Updated November 2026 · 6 min read
We make REPS, so we are not neutral.

If you are leaving Recall to actually remember what you save, REPS, the AI companion we make, is our pick: you tap the ideas you want from each save and they come back as a short daily game on iPhone. Recall (recall.it) is still the stronger library: it saves and summarizes, and on its paid plans connects, chats and quizzes, on every platform. Choose REPS for remembering, Recl for lighter video summaries, Readwise for highlights, Gemini Notebook for research, Obsidian for notes you own and Anki for cards you write.

Checked on each app's own site and pricing page on 24 November 2026; Recall facts are from recall.it, its pricing page and quiz docs. Features and prices change.

What you would give up

Recall runs on the web, iOS, Android and four browsers, summarizes YouTube videos, podcasts, articles and PDFs, exports to Markdown and offers API and MCP access on every plan, free included. Plus adds the automatic graph, chat, bulk imports and quizzes you can schedule; Max adds the choice of AI model (pricing). See REPS vs Recall.

The six alternatives

REPS: when you want it to come back to you

Our pick, for remembering. Save an article, a YouTube video, a podcast episode, a PDF or a photo of a book page. REPS pulls out the ideas worth keeping, you tap the ones you want, now or later, and they come back in a short daily session in four formats, timed with spaced repetition, through six levels from New to Locked In. Ladder, Crossword and Cryptic are built from your saves, Quotle is a daily quote game, and Friends Trivia lets you play the same ten rounds as a friend (REPS facts). A Discover feed lists new articles to explore, each showing how many concepts it holds.

Recl: video-first, with summaries and reviews

The lighter option. Summarizes YouTube videos, reels, TikToks, X posts and articles, brings each save back after 1, 3, 7, 14, 30 and 60 days (a tap marks it reviewed) and writes a weekly quiz. It has Ask with sources, a knowledge graph, 20 app languages and an MCP server; it is very new, and the web app needs a paid plan (Recl, pricing).

Readwise: the best home for highlights

For readers. Reader saves articles, PDFs, EPUBs, newsletters, RSS and YouTube transcripts; Readwise imports highlights from Kindle, Apple Books, Kobo and 40-plus sources, exports to Notion and Obsidian, and brings them back in a Daily Review. It remembers what you highlight, not the whole source, and is paid after a 30-day trial (Reader, pricing).

Gemini Notebook: deep questions about one set of sources

For research. Google's tool, previously NotebookLM, takes PDFs, web pages, public YouTube links, audio and Google Docs, answers questions, makes Audio Overviews and mind maps, and generates flashcards or quizzes when you ask. Each notebook stands alone, and Google's help does not describe a review schedule (sources, flashcards and quizzes).

Obsidian: notes you own, with a graph

For your own notes. Stores notes privately on your device in open file formats, has thousands of plugins and is free with no sign-up, with optional paid Sync and Publish. Recall's Markdown export opens in it, but it does not summarize or quiz you out of the box (Obsidian).

Anki: flashcards you build and tune

For full control. Free, open-source flashcards scheduled for when you are most likely to forget, with free AnkiWeb sync. AnkiDroid is free; AnkiMobile for iPhone is paid. You write every card yourself (Anki).

Where REPS fits better

  • You only ever used Recall to remember things, and want that as a few minutes of questions a day.
  • You want it to feel like a game, with word games made from your saves.
  • You want friends in it: Friends Trivia on the same ten rounds, activity and a leaderboard.
  • You save books as photos of pages, or a reading list from a screenshot or a Goodreads export. Get the app.

Where Recall fits better

  • You use its summaries, chat and graph every week, and the only problem is forgetting to quiz yourself: on Plus, generating questions for the cards that matter and switching on "Schedule for review" gives you spaced repetition inside Recall.
  • You need one library on every platform, with exports, API and MCP access, chat across the web (Plus) and a choice of AI model (Max).

Where REPS is weaker

  • It does less than Recall as a library: Ask Echo covers what you have saved, not the open web.
  • The app is iPhone only, with a Chrome extension for saving: no Android app, no web app, and no offline use.
  • No library imports from other apps, only a reading list from a screenshot or a Goodreads export.
FAQ

Frequently asked

What is the best alternative to Recall?

It depends on why you are leaving. For remembering what you save as a daily game on iPhone, REPS: you pick the key ideas and it builds the questions. For a lighter, video-first app, Recl. For reading and highlighting, Readwise. For research on one topic, Gemini Notebook. For notes you own as files, Obsidian. For building your own flashcards, Anki.

Is there a free alternative to Recall?

Obsidian is free with no sign-up, with optional paid Sync and Publish. Anki is open source, AnkiDroid on Android is free, and AnkiWeb sync is free. Gemini Notebook has a free tier with a Google account. REPS is currently free. Recall's own free plan has 10 AI summaries a month; its quizzes are on the paid Plus plan.

Which Recall alternative has spaced repetition?

REPS, Recl, Readwise and Anki all bring things back on a schedule. REPS quizzes you daily on the ideas you pick, Recl brings saves back after 1, 3, 7, 14, 30 and 60 days, Readwise reviews your highlights, and Anki schedules cards you write. Gemini Notebook can generate flashcards and quizzes, but Google's help does not describe a review schedule.

Is Recall the same as Microsoft Recall or Recl?

No. Recall (recall.it, formerly getrecall.ai) is a personal AI knowledge base. Microsoft Recall is a Windows 11 feature, and Recl (recl.app) is a separate, newer app from a different team.

Can I export my data from Recall?

Yes. Recall lets you export everything as zipped Markdown files from Settings. Obsidian can open Markdown files directly. REPS does not import your library from other apps, so you would save what you want to keep into REPS yourself.
