Where it leads

Every point of light is something one person chose to keep.

Over weeks, what you save starts to connect. The book finds the podcast, the podcast finds the article you nearly lost. Turn it, zoom in, and see the shape of what you know. Then ask Echo, your companion in the app, about any of it.

How it works

Three steps from forgotten to yours.

You already read the long piece, watched the documentary, finished the book. REPS takes it from there.

1

Save it from wherever you are.

Tap Share in Safari, YouTube, Spotify, Instagram or Apple Podcasts and choose REPS. Add a book by its title, snap your bookshelf, drop in a PDF, or type a note. That's your whole job.

2

Keep the ideas worth keeping.

REPS pulls out the ideas. You choose the ones you care about, and they join your library beside the books, talks and articles you've already kept. You decide what stays.

3

It comes back as a daily game.

A few minutes a day, made from your own material, and each idea returns just as it starts to fade. Play Ladder, Crossword, Cryptic and Quotle, or Friends Trivia when you'd rather play with someone.

Save one thing tonight. Play it tomorrow.

What you end up with

Months later, it's still yours.

The mountain book you couldn't put down. The interview that changed how you talk to people. The documentary you'd have half-forgotten by Friday.

Not a pile of bookmarks. A library you can actually draw on: at dinner, at work, mid-conversation, when someone says “didn't you read something about that?”

Why this exists

“I'm building the antidote.”

Mike McGraw spent twelve years investing in technology, latterly at Inovia Capital, where he opened the European office. Then he watched his nephews and nieces grow up on YouTube Shorts, and read that the Flynn effect has gone into reverse. He thinks Huxley called it, not Orwell: control through entertainment that asks nothing of you.

So he deleted Instagram, went back to books, and on 30 June 2026, his 34th birthday, left to build REPS full-time. He uses it every day.
